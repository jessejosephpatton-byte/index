Black Manhattan
Rye · Averna or Barolo Chinato · Angostura · Orange Bitters
Stirred
▼
2 ozRittenhouse 100 Rye
1 ozAverna amaro or Cocchi Barolo Chinato (premium)
2 dashAngostura bitters
1 dashOrange bitters
3 dropsSaline solution
garnishLuxardo cherry + optional orange twist
01
Combine
Add all ingredients to a mixing glass with ice.
02
Stir
Stir 25–30 seconds until well chilled and diluted.
03
Strain & Garnish
Strain into chilled coupe or over large cube. Express orange twist. Drop in cherry.
Notes
Averna build — everyday version, herbal and approachable. Barolo Chinato build — premium version, more bitter, more complex (proven excellent at Moody Tongue). Use high-proof rye — low-proof bourbon gets lost against amaro. Luxardo cherries only. Saline suppresses bitterness and amplifies other flavors — don't skip it.
Vieux Carré
Rye · Cognac · Chinato · Bénédictine · Peychaud's · Angostura
Stirred
▼
¾ ozRittenhouse 100 Rye
¾ ozCognac (Rémy Martin VSOP)
¾ ozCocchi Barolo Chinato or Cocchi di Torino
¼ ozBénédictine
2 dashPeychaud's bitters
1 dashAngostura bitters
garnishLemon twist
01
Combine
Add all ingredients to a mixing glass with ice.
02
Stir — Long
Stir 30–35 seconds. Longer than a standard Manhattan — this one needs it.
03
Strain & Garnish
Strain over large cube (traditional) or into chilled coupe. Express lemon twist over top, run around rim.
Notes
Born at Moody Tongue with Barolo Chinato — the bartender's call that made the drink. Don't skip Peychaud's — its anise-tinged floral quality is the soul of this drink. Angostura alone is not a substitute. The rye + cognac split achieves complexity neither spirit manages alone.
The Winnequah
Rye · Laphroaig · Barolo Chinato · Bénédictine · Peychaud's · Absinthe
Original · Rocks
▼
1½ ozRittenhouse 100 Rye
¾ ozLaphroaig 10 (or cask strength for more smoke)
¾ ozCocchi Barolo Chinato
¼ ozBénédictine
2 dashPeychaud's bitters
3 dropsAbsinthe (drops — not dashes)
3 dropsSaline solution
garnishOrange twist (essential)
01
Chill Glass
Chill your rocks glass in the freezer or with ice water while you build the drink.
02
Combine
Add all ingredients to a mixing glass with ice. The absinthe is drops — 3 maximum. If you can taste it, you used too much.
03
Stir — Full 40s
Stir for a full 35–40 seconds. This is a big drink and needs real integration and dilution.
04
Strain & Serve
Drop large clear cube in chilled glass. Strain over the cube.
05
Garnish
Express orange twist close to the surface — oils hit the drink, not the air. Run around rim. Drop in. The orange oil is structural, not decorative.
Notes
Named for Winnequah Road, Monona. Rye + Laphroaig split base — smoke and spice as co-equals, not float and base. Barolo Chinato is the binding agent; don't substitute standard sweet vermouth. Peychaud's anise thread connects the smoke to the absinthe whisper. Cask strength Laphroaig = wilder. Standard = more integrated. Both correct.

Penicillin
Blended Scotch · Lemon · Honey-Ginger · Laphroaig Float
Shaken · Rocks
▼
2 ozBlended Scotch (Famous Grouse)
¾ ozFresh lemon juice
¾ ozHoney-ginger syrup*
¼ ozLaphroaig 10 (float)
01
Combine Base
Add Scotch, lemon, and honey-ginger syrup to shaker with ice.
02
Shake Hard
Shake 12–15 seconds.
03
Strain
Strain over large cube in rocks glass.
04
Float the Islay
Pour Laphroaig gently over the back of a bar spoon. It should sit on top — the smoke hits your nose first.
Notes
*Honey-ginger syrup: simmer 1 cup honey + 1 cup water + 6–8 slices fresh ginger 10 min, strain. Refrigerate up to 2 weeks. Works in tea, dark & stormy riffs too. The Laphroaig float is structural — it makes the first sip essentially an Old Fashioned. The sour elements are backbone, not headline.

Smoked Reposado Margarita
Reposado · Mezcal · Lime · Cointreau · Agave
Shaken · Rocks
▼
1½ ozReposado tequila (La Gritona)
½ ozMezcal espadin (El Buho or Del Maguey)
¾ ozFresh lime juice (never bottled)
½ ozCointreau (not triple sec)
¼ ozAgave syrup (2:1)
3 dropsSaline solution
rimKosher salt — half rim only
garnishLime wheel or expressed lime twist
01
Salt Rim
Run lime wedge around half the rim only. Dip in kosher salt. Half rim lets you control each sip.
02
Combine
Add all ingredients to shaker with ice.
03
Shake Hard
Shake 12–15 seconds. Don't under-shake.
04
Strain & Garnish
Strain over fresh ice in rocks glass. Express lime twist over top.
Notes
Smoky split: ¼ oz mezcal = subtle smoke. Equal parts (1 oz / 1 oz) = bold statement. Your mood decides. Cointreau is cognac-based — drier and more complex than triple sec. Saline both in the shaker and on the half rim — they serve different roles. Sweetness floor is ¼ oz agave; taste your lime first before adding more.

For Friends — Plus Your Twist
Espresso Martini
Vodka · Coffee Liqueur · Espresso
Shaken · Up
▼
1½ ozVodka (standard) or 1 oz vodka + ½ oz rye (your version)
¾ ozKahlúa (standard) or Mr. Black Cold Brew (upgraded / your version)
1 ozFresh espresso shot (room temp or chilled)
¼ ozSimple syrup — omit if using Mr. Black
1 dashAngostura — your version only, optional
garnish3 coffee beans, clustered on foam
01
Pull & Cool Espresso
Pull one shot and let cool 2–3 minutes. Hot liquid kills the foam.
02
Combine
Add all ingredients to shaker with ice.
03
Shake Very Hard
15–18 seconds, more vigorous than any other drink. The foam is structural, not decorative.
04
Double Strain
Pour deliberately through strainer — let the foam build on top, don't trap it in the strainer.
05
Garnish Fast
Place 3 coffee beans in a tight cluster on the foam. Serve immediately.
Three Versions
Standard (friends): Vodka + Kahlúa + espresso + simple syrup. Classic, reliable, crowd-pleasing.

Upgraded (friends, noticeably better): Swap Kahlúa for Mr. Black, drop simple syrup — Mr. Black has enough sweetness without it. Mr. Black is genuinely coffee-forward; Kahlúa is coffee-flavored sugar syrup.

Your version: 1 oz rye + ½ oz vodka, Mr. Black, espresso, no syrup, optional dash Angostura. Bold, bitter, barely sweet — a genuinely different drink.

No espresso machine? Best: Moka pot (~$30, Bialetti) — produces real crema, foams well. Good: store-bought cold brew concentrate (Chameleon, Trader Joe's) — 1 oz in place of shot, no foam, still tastes great. Functional: double-strength drip coffee, cooled — minimal foam. A Moka pot solves this permanently for $30 and is worth owning.
Gold Rush
Bourbon · Lemon · Honey Syrup
Shaken · Rocks
▼
2 ozBourbon (Buffalo Trace standard; Rittenhouse or Whistlepig rye for your version)
¾ ozFresh lemon juice
¾ ozHoney syrup (3:1 honey to warm water)*
¼ ozBénédictine float — your version only
3 dropsSaline solution — your version only
garnishLemon twist or wheel
01
Combine
Add bourbon, lemon, honey syrup, and saline (if your version) to shaker with ice.
02
Shake
Shake hard 12–15 seconds.
03
Strain
Strain over fresh ice in rocks glass.
04
Float (Your Version)
Pour Bénédictine over back of bar spoon so it rests on top. Garnish with lemon.
Notes
*Honey syrup: 3:1 ratio (3 parts honey, 1 part warm water) — richer and more honey-forward than standard simple syrup. Refrigerate up to 3 weeks. Worth making a batch.

Your version: High-proof rye instead of bourbon, saline in the shaker, Bénédictine float. The Bénédictine echoes the honey but adds herbal depth — same float technique as the Laphroaig in the Penicillin. Barely more effort, noticeably more interesting.
Gin & Tonic
Dry Gin · Tonic · Lime
Built · Highball
▼
2 ozDry gin (Tanqueray standard; Sølveig for local twist) or 1½ oz gin for your version
½ ozMezcal espadin — your version only (El Buho or Del Maguey)
4 ozFever-Tree Indian Tonic (standard) or Fever-Tree Aromatic (your version)
2 dashOrange bitters — your version only
garnishLime wedge (standard) or grapefruit twist (your version)
01
Fill Glass
Fill tall highball with ice. For your version, one large cube — less surface area, slower dilution, longer carbonation life.
02
Add Spirit(s)
Add gin (and mezcal if your version) over ice.
03
Add Tonic
Pour tonic slowly down the side of the glass to preserve carbonation. One gentle stir only.
04
Bitters & Garnish
For your version: add orange bitters on top, they drift down naturally. Express grapefruit twist close to the surface, place on rim.
Notes
Standard (friends): 2 oz gin, 4 oz Fever-Tree Indian Tonic, lime wedge. Build in the glass — never shake.

Your version (Mezcal Tonic): 1½ oz gin + ½ oz mezcal, Fever-Tree Aromatic Tonic (more bitter, more herbal, less sweet than Indian), orange bitters, grapefruit twist. Smoke and quinine bitterness amplify each other in exactly the direction your palate responds to.

Sølveig note: The Fár North Spirits gin from your butcher shop shelf is 107 proof — reduce tonic slightly to compensate for the extra spirit weight.
Profile
Whiskey-forward and unapologetically spirit-led. Drawn to bold, assertive modifiers — amaro, Campari, Fernet, Bénédictine — that announce themselves rather than whisper. Bitters are essential, not optional. Sweetness is welcome only as counterbalance, never as the point. Smoke is consistently compelling: Laphroaig, mezcal, peated Scotch. Even sour-format drinks work when the smoke and bitterness lead and citrus/honey play a structural rather than starring role. Barrel-aged formats are a natural fit.

Origin Story
The Winnequah was born at Moody Tongue, Chicago — where a bartender introduced Cocchi Barolo Chinato, built a Vieux Carré, and made a Black Manhattan that became an instant favorite. The Aviary provided the barrel-aged context. Both are worth returning to; Moody Tongue is the place you go back to.
